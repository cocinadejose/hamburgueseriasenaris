The Most Important Festivals in Galicia

As a lifelong compostelana and co-owner of the Hamburguesería Señarís, the Apostle Festivals are the most special of the year for me. Santiago transforms completely: the streets fill with light, music, and people from all over the world who come to celebrate with us, including many Camino pilgrims who arrive just for these dates.

In this guide I'm going to tell you everything you need to know to experience the Apostle Santiago Festivals 2027 like a local. Not just dates and times, but the tips that only someone who lives here year-round can give you.

✅ What we already know about the 2027 Festivals (and what we don't)

Confirmed: the festivals run from July 15 to 31, 2027, with the main day on July 25 (Galicia Day). And because that date falls on a Sunday, 2027 is a Compostela Holy Year.

Still to be officially published: the full programme of concerts and activities, the poster, the exact times and the Holy Year novelties. Santiago City Council usually publishes the programme in spring.

Official sources: Concello de Santiago de Compostela, Cathedral of Santiago (catedraldesantiago.es) and Turismo de Galicia. As soon as the 2027 programme is out, we will update this guide.

⭐ Compostela Holy Year: why 2027 is special

Every so often, July 25 — the feast of Saint James the Apostle — falls on a Sunday. When that happens, we celebrate a Compostela Holy Year (also called a Jubilee Year or Xacobeo). And in 2027 it happens again. For us it is no ordinary year: you can feel it in the streets, in the number of pilgrims, and in festivals that grow in every direction.

This is not decided on a whim — it goes back a long way. The Holy Year dates back to 1122, under Pope Callixtus II, and was fixed for good by Alexander III's bull Regis aeterni in 1179. It repeats on a cycle of 11, 6, 5 and 6 years; the previous one was 2021-2022, extended because of the pandemic. The next one is this: 2027.

📌 Confirmed facts about the 2027 Holy Year

  • Why: July 25, 2027 falls on a Sunday.
  • Official motto: "When they saw the star, they rejoiced with great joy" (Al ver la estrella se llenaron de inmensa alegría).
  • Official hymn: Un campo cheo de estrelas.
  • Plenary indulgence (the "Jubilee"): visit the Apostle's tomb in the Cathedral and say a prayer, go to confession (within 15 days before or after) and receive communion. It can be gained once a day and may be applied to the deceased.
  • Note: neither the Jubilee grace nor the pilgrimage depends on the Compostela certificate. You can gain the Jubilee by travelling however you like, and you can receive the Compostela without gaining the Jubilee.

Source: official website of the Cathedral of Santiago de Compostela (Holy Year section).

Local tip: if you dream of coming in a Holy Year, don't leave it to the last minute. It is the year when the most pilgrims gather and accommodation goes fast. There is still no official date for the opening of the Holy Door or a programme of events: as soon as it is published, you will find it here.

📋 Full Programme of the Festivals

The Apostle Festivals last 17 days (July 15-31) and offer a varied programme for all tastes. This is the pattern of the main events, as they have been held in recent editions. The official 2027 programme is published by Santiago City Council in spring.

🎇 Fireworks of the Apostle - July 24, around 23:30h

The most important pyrotechnic show in Galicia. In recent editions it has been about 45 minutes of fireworks launched from Monte do Gozo, synchronized with music and a drone show.

Duration: Approximately 45 minutes

Access: Free and visible from multiple points in the city

2027 time and staging pending official confirmation.

⛪ Solemn Mass and National Offering - July 25

The most important religious event. In recent editions the Mass has begun at 10:30h in the Cathedral, followed by the National Offering around 12:00h.

Presided by: the Archbishop of Santiago, with the official representation of the State (usually the King or his delegate).

Botafumeiro: possible use (not guaranteed)

Access: Free, but arriving very early recommended (at dawn)

2027 timetable pending confirmation by the Cathedral Chapter.

🎭 Other Main Events

These are the events that come round almost every year (dates from the usual pattern, pending the official 2027 programme):

  • Festival Proclamation: July 15 - Plaza del Obradoiro
  • Medieval Market: mid-July - Plaza de Abastos and old town
  • Craft Fair (Trad Fest): July 20-27 - Plaza de Mazarelos
  • Festa Pícara (children): July 22 - Parque Eugenio Granell
  • Giants and Big-Heads: July 24 and 25 - Various routes
  • Daily Concerts: July 17-31 - Quintana, Alameda, Galeras, Monte do Gozo
  • Closing Fireworks: July 31 - Alameda

🎇 Fireworks of the Apostle: The Most Anticipated Show

The night of July 24 is, for many compostelanos, the most magical night of the year. Santiago's sky lights up with a unique pyrotechnic spectacle.

Every year, on the night of July 24, my family and I prepare dinner early and head to the Seminario Menor Gardens in Belvís. This has been our ritual for years. We arrive around 22:30h, find a good spot on the grass, and wait among laughter and chats with neighbours. When the first fireworks begin, silence falls over the whole area. You only hear the "ohhh" and "ahhh" of the crowd. And at the end, during the finale, everyone applauds. It's something that can't be explained in words, you have to experience it. From our area (Belvís) the views are privileged. In fact, my daughter recorded last year's finale from there. If you want to see what it's like, you'll find the video below.

⛪ Solemn Mass and National Offering

July 25 is the main day. In the morning, the Cathedral hosts the most important religious acts of the festivals.

Religious Programme for July 25

Time Event Details
10:30h Solemn Mass of the Apostle Santiago Cathedral
11:45h Procession of the Saint Cathedral Interior
12:00h National Offering Main Chapel
The National Offering is a unique act in Spain. The King (or his representative) offers the Apostle the "regalia": a symbolic gift representing the devotion of all Spain to Santiago. It's a solemn, moving act with brutal historical weight. If you want to enter the Cathedral that day, my advice is to get up very early. The queues start forming at 7-8 in the morning. And even if you don't get in, the atmosphere on the Obradoiro is worth it.
Botafumeiro? The famous giant incense burner may be used during the solemn Mass, but it's not guaranteed. It depends on the Cathedral Chapter and funding on that day. If you're lucky enough to see it, it's an unforgettable experience.

🎵 Concerts and Musical Performances

During the two festival weeks, Santiago fills with music. There are concerts for all tastes at different venues around the city. For more information about the Camino de Santiago and where to stay during your visit, consult our complete guide for pilgrims.

📍 Main Venues

  • Plaza de la Quintana: Galician, national and international music concerts. Free access until capacity is reached.
  • Alameda Park: Traditional verbenas, orchestras, dance music.
  • Galeras Park: Family concerts, children's activities.
  • Plaza de Mazarelos: Traditional Galician music, bagpipes, folklore.
  • Monte do Gozo: Major concerts (national and international artists).

🎤 Concerts and artists: 2027 programme still to come

The full 2027 concert programme has not been published yet: Santiago City Council releases it in spring. The names below are the ones that usually play at the Apostle Festivals, based on recent editions:

  • Verbena Orchestras: Orquesta Galilea, Orquesta Alianza, Orquesta Miramar, Los Coleguitas
  • Galician Groups: Sés, Baiuca, Mercedes Peón, A Banda da Balboa
  • National Artists: Amaral, Vetusta Morla, Izal
  • Traditional Music: Real Filharmonía de Galicia, Banda Municipal de Música

📅 Full official programme: will be published in spring at santiagoturismo.com and on the City Council website. As soon as it is out, we will update it here.

Tip: Concerts at Quintana and Alameda usually start at 22:00-23:00h. Arrive early if you want to get a spot near the stage.

🥘 Festival Gastronomy

The festivals wouldn't be the same without the food. Galicia has exceptional gastronomy, and during these dates there are products you can't miss. If you want to explore more about Galicia's gastronomic festivals, consult our complete guide to Galicia's gastronomic festivals.

🍽️ Typical Dishes for These Dates

Dish Description Where to try
Pulpo a feira Cooked octopus with paprika, salt and oil. Served with cachelos (potatoes). Alameda stalls, centre pulperías
Empanada gallega Pastry filled with tuna, meat, scallops or cod. Plaza de Abastos, traditional bakeries
Tarta de Santiago Traditional dessert of almond, egg and sugar with the Santiago Cross. Centre bakeries, competition on Obradoiro
Albariño D.O. White wine from Rías Baixas, perfect for seafood. Wine cellars and old town bars
Spanish Tortilla In sandwich or whole. Our speciality (to order) 😊 🍔 Hamburguesería Señarís

🏪 Markets and Gastronomic Fairs

  • Medieval Market: July 18-20 at Plaza de Abastos. Artisan products, tastings, medieval atmosphere.
  • Craft Fair (Trad Fest): July 20-27 at Mazarelos. Galician crafts, traditional food, workshops.
  • Tarta de Santiago Competition: July 25 at Plaza del Obradoiro. Public tasting of finalist cakes.
The Medieval Market of Plaza de Abastos is one of my favourite spots during the festivals. The stalls are decorated with medieval fabrics, there's bagpipe music and you can taste artisan cheeses, cured meats, honey... It's like travelling back in time. And if you like Tarta de Santiago, July 25 is your day. On the Obradoiro they set up a stage where bakeries compete for the best cake. You can taste them all and vote for your favourite. It's free!
Dining times: In Galicia we eat late. Restaurants fill up at 14:30h for lunch and 21:30h for dinner. If you want to avoid queues, go earlier (13:30h / 20:30h) or later (15:30h / 22:30h).

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 Activities for Families with Children

The Apostle Festivals are perfect for enjoying with the family. There are specific activities for the little ones throughout the two weeks.

🎪 Featured Activities for Children

  • Festa Pícara: July 22, 12:00h - Parque Eugenio Granell. Games, workshops, children's shows.
  • Giants and Big-Heads: July 24 and 25. Routes through the old town (check times).
  • Comparsa de Cabezudos: July 25, 12:00h - Departure from Teatro Principal.
  • Craft Workshops: July 20-27 - Plaza de Mazarelos. Bagpipe, traditional dance, craft workshops.
  • Storytelling: Various days - Biblioteca Ánxel Casal. Stories about Santiago and the Camino.
My daughter, nephews and friends love the big-heads. They come out in the afternoons, around 18:00-19:00h, and tour the streets of the old town. The kids go crazy watching them dance and chase them. It's a classic that never goes out of style. The Festa Pícara on July 22 is also great. There are bouncy castles, face painting, workshops... The kids have a great time and parents can rest a while on the park benches.
⚠️ IMPORTANT - July 25: Avoid the old town with small children on the main day. The overcrowding is brutal and it's very easy to lose sight of them. Better stay around Alameda or quieter areas.

💡 Practical Tips from a Compostelana

After experiencing the Apostle Festivals all my life, I've learned a few things that I think can help you enjoy your visit more. And since visitor numbers increase every year, these tips are more useful than ever:

🏨 Accommodation

  • Book WELL in advance. I'm talking 3-6 months before, especially if you're coming the weekend of July 24-26.
  • If you don't find anything in Santiago, look in nearby towns: Padrón (15 min), Teo (10 min), Ames (10 min). All well connected by bus.
  • Pilgrim hostels usually fill up. If you want a hostel, call before coming. For more information, consult our complete guide for pilgrims.
  • Prices skyrocket during these dates. A hotel that costs €80 in July can cost €150 during the festivals.

🚗 Transport and Mobility

  • ⚠️ MY ADVICE AND ABSOLUTE RECOMMENDATION: Come without a car. During the festivals the centre is chaos. Parking is impossible and traffic jams are kilometres long. Coming by car to these overcrowded festivals guarantees going round and round at a snail's pace looking for parking. It's better to leave the car in a nearby town or at friends' or family's house than to get in the car looking for parking in the city.
  • The old town is 100% pedestrian. Everything can be done on foot.
  • City bus: Runs all night during the festivals (special night lines).
  • Taxi: Difficult to get during the festivals. Remember my husband is a taxi driver! 😊 Call the local and I'll tell you if he's available.

🍽️ Where to Eat

  • Book ALWAYS. Restaurants fill up. Call at least 2-3 days in advance.
  • If you haven't booked, get away from Rúa do Franco and Obradoiro. In neighbourhoods like San Pedro, Conxo or Santa Marta you'll find quieter places.
  • The tapas bars in the old town are packed. Go early (13:00h / 20:00h) or late (15:30h / 22:30h).
  • The Alameda stalls serve pulpo and empanada during the festivals. It's cheap, authentic and delicious.
  • And you know, if you want good homemade burgers in a relaxed atmosphere, you know where we are 😉

🌧️ The Weather

  • July is one of the driest months, but it can always rain in Santiago. Always bring an umbrella or light raincoat.
  • Temperatures: 20-28°C during the day, 12-16°C at night. Light clothing but with something warm for the night.
  • The fireworks on July 24 are outdoors. If it's cold or rains, you might want a jacket.
