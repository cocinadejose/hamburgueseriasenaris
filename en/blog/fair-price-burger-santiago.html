How Can You Sell a Burger for €3 in Santiago?

Published 29 June 2026 | By Susana | ⏰ Last updated: 29 June 2026

Note: When I say "Santiago" I mean Santiago de Compostela, the historic capital of Galicia in northwest Spain — world-famous as the final destination of the Camino de Santiago, the ancient pilgrimage route. It is a small, walkable city with a magnificent medieval cathedral at its heart, a busy old town packed with visitors, and quiet residential neighbourhoods just a short stroll away.

The Question We Get Asked All the Time

Today I want to answer something people ask us over and over again, especially when they compare our prices with other places around the city — or even further afield. I'll break it down into three clear parts: the building itself, what goes into the food, and the way we choose to do business.

There is no catch, no trick, no hidden shortcut. What there is, is hard work, deliberate choices, and a way of running a hospitality business that is perhaps not the standard one these days — but it is ours. Let me explain.

🔍 Complete Transparency

This is not a clever marketing article. It is an honest reply to a real question customers ask us every single week. If you are reading this, you have almost certainly wondered the same thing.

1. The Premises

We Don't Pay Rent — But That Is Only Half the Story

It is absolutely true: we own this place outright, so there is no monthly rent bill. But that is far from the whole story. This building is ours only because Antonio and I worked ourselves into the ground for years, paying off the bank loan month after month — no holidays, no luxuries, no time off. It was a marathon, not a sprint.

Now that the loan is fully cleared, our fixed costs are very lean: electricity, water, gas, business taxes and insurance, and that is basically it. We have no expensive glass facades to maintain, no stylish decor that has to be replaced every couple of years to keep up with trends, no huge start-up investment still needing to be paid back.

I remember those years clearly. Antonio and I would take turns behind the counter from 8 o'clock in the morning until midnight. We never had summers off, we never took long weekends, we never put a sign on the door saying "closed for a break". Every single euro that came in went straight towards clearing that debt with the bank. Nowadays when people say to me "you're so lucky you don't have to pay rent", I just smile and think to myself — luck had nothing to do with it. It was sheer, relentless work.

A Neighbourhood Location — Not a Picture-Postcard Address

We are on Avenida de Quiroga Palacios, about a five-minute walk from the Plaza de Abastos — Santiago's historic covered fresh food market, the beating heart of local food shopping in the city since the 19th century. This is a real residential area, where people actually live and work. We are not right in the tourist zone immediately around the Cathedral, where rent per square metre can easily be five times higher — sometimes even more. We are simply not paying a premium just to feature on a postcard.

Summary on premises: We own the building, everything is kept simple, and we trade in a residential neighbourhood rather than the expensive tourist centre. All those savings are passed directly to you in the price you pay. It is not magic. It is simply that our overheads do not strangle us.

2. The Food

Beef Straight from the Market — Nothing Hidden

I go personally to buy our meat at the Plaza de Abastos, from Carlos's butcher stall. Anyone can go there and buy exactly the same meat, see the price tags with their own eyes, check everything for themselves. There are no secrets, no special deals nobody else can get.

It is 100% Galician beef, reared here in the region. We do not use meat carrying the official Ternera Gallega seal — that is the European protected designation-of-origin quality label for premium Galician veal, and using it alone would increase our cost by 50% or more. Even without that certification, however, this is excellent local meat: no additives, no preservatives, no fancy flavourings mixed in. The butcher minces it twice, following an exact ratio we were taught 25 years ago by an old-time market trader. He has long since retired, but Carlos's butchery still prepares it exactly the same way for us today.

Working like this — with meat delivered fresh every morning and hand-formed into patties right here in the shop — means we control every step of quality, and we can keep prices fair without ever cutting corners.

If you want all the technical details — the cuts we use, the exact ratios, and how to ask your own butcher for the same — I've written a full guide to our beef.

Absolute Simplicity

What comes off our hotplate is completely unadorned: beef and salt — nothing else. Our basic burger really is just that: good meat in a good bun. Alongside it you get individual sachets of mustard, ketchup and mayonnaise — you dress it exactly how you like it. We will never judge anyone for how they prefer to eat their burger. Black pepper and other seasonings are also there on the counter if you want them. But at its heart, it is simply beef and salt.

When your raw ingredients are good and you treat them with respect, you do not need to disguise them with layers of extras. The flavour speaks for itself.

You can read more about our approach in my article: The Perfect Burger — Great Taste and Wholesome Food.

🥩 The Ingredients Are Not the Secret

Santiago is a small place. Many restaurants — including other burger bars — buy their meat from exactly the same stall at the market. Same product, same supplier. So as you can see, the food itself is not where the difference lies. Every business chooses its own model. Ours is simply: good honest ingredients, prepared properly, at a fair price.

3. How We Run This Business — Our Values

No Plans to Grow Into a Big Chain

We have zero ambition to expand, open more branches or become a franchise brand. We are from here. This is our home. We have done our travelling, seen the world, lived our adventures — we feel very fortunate and fulfilled already. Our greatest pleasure now is that our neighbours and friends come to see us, and we never have to leave our own community to make a living.

Two People, Two Personalities, One Shared Commitment

Antonio is a free spirit at heart. He loves swapping stories, chatting to everyone who walks in, and listening to the experiences of pilgrims — the people walking the Camino de Santiago, the long-distance pilgrimage that finishes at the Cathedral barely ten minutes' walk from here. That warmth comes through naturally, not just in the shop but also here on the website, where he loves writing helpful articles for anyone travelling the Camino or discovering the city. It is a genuine calling for him.

I'm Susana, and I'm more rooted in the everyday life of the neighbourhood. I spend less time reading philosophy or thinking about cultural experiences, but I care just as deeply about making absolutely everyone who steps through the door feel like they are visiting friends. My way is perhaps more down-to-earth, but it comes from exactly the same place.

Every customer who walks through that door is a guest, a friend coming to call. Of course we need to make a profit — enough to keep the doors open and to live a decent, comfortable life on our own terms. But we do not get up in the morning thinking about turnover figures or profit margins. We get up wondering who will come in today, what stories they will bring, and whether today's batch of burgers will taste every bit as good as yesterday's.

🗣️ Spanish Phrases for Ordering at Bars and Restaurants

If you are visiting Santiago from abroad, a few words of Spanish go a very, very long way. Nobody expects perfect grammar — but trying is always appreciated. Here are the phrases you will actually use when ordering food and drinks around town (including at our place).

🇪🇸 SPANISH🇬🇧 ENGLISH
🍔 ORDERING AT A BURGER BAR / CAFETERÍA
Una hamburguesa simple, por favorA basic burger, please
Una hamburguesa completa / especialA fully loaded burger / special burger
¿Qué lleva la hamburguesa especial?What comes on the special burger?
Sin cebolla / Sin lechuga, por favorNo onion / No lettuce, please
Muy hecho / Al punto / Poco hechoWell done / Medium / Rare
Para llevar / Para comer aquíTo take away / To eat here
Una ración de patatas fritas, por favorA portion of chips, please
🍻 DRINKS
Una caña, por favorA small draft beer, please
Un agua con gas / sin gasSparkling / Still water
Un vino tinto / blancoA red / white wine
La cuenta, por favorThe bill, please
🙏 BEING POLITE
Gracias / Muchas graciasThank you / Thank you very much
Por favorPlease
Buenos días / Buenas tardes / Buenas nochesGood morning / Good afternoon / Good evening
Hasta luegoSee you later
Está buenísimo / Muy buenoIt's absolutely delicious / Very good
Pronunciation tip: In Galicia and across most of Spain, "c" before "e" or "i" and "z" are pronounced like the "th" in "think" — not like an "s". So cerveza (beer) sounds roughly like "ther-VE-tha", and gracias sounds like "GRA-thyas". Using it will instantly make you sound like you know what you're doing.
