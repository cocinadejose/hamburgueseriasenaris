Galicians are big on gastronomic festivals. In our case, Susana isn't so fond of crowds, but I, Antonio, have spent years visiting all the gastronomic festivals I know in Galicia and even sometimes beyond, especially in northern Portugal, where gastronomy has that Atlantic brotherhood that unites us.

Today I'm going to list the dates of the most prestigious and famous gastronomic festivals in Galicia, as well as elaborate plans so travelers can make their vacations coincide with our land's gastronomic offerings. Generally, these festivals don't vary their dates, but we'll focus on 2026. For greater certainty, verify the data with town halls as dates approach.

⚠️ Practical Advice: Accommodation and Transportation

During the most popular festivals, especially in July and August, make sure you have accommodation booked. Although it doesn't necessarily have to be at the event location: you can use public transport from one city to another or to a nearby town. This way, apart from saving money, you may avoid accommodation problems. Galicia has a good network of buses and trains connecting the main towns.

⚠️ Important: Tentative Dates

The exact dates of many festivals are confirmed a few months in advance. We give you the usual dates based on previous years, but we recommend confirming on official websites as the date approaches. Most are celebrated on weekends near the indicated dates.

The 10 Must-See Gastronomic Festivals of Galicia

If you can only attend a few, these are the ones you can't miss:

🍷 1. Cambados Wine Festival (Albariño Festival)

Where: Cambados, Pontevedra
When: First weekend of August (August 4-6, 2026)
What to expect: The queen of Galician festivals. Three days of tastings, concerts, markets and, of course, lots of Albariño. Saturday is the big day with the "Great Wine Festival" where thousands of people toast together.

Local tip: Arrive early on Saturday to get a spot at the best tastings. And don't miss the traditional food stalls that accompany the wine.

🥘 2. Lalín Stew Festival

Where: Lalín, Pontevedra
When: Last weekend of February (February 27 - March 1, 2026)
What to expect: Galicia's most famous stew. Thousands of portions served in the street, stew contest, gastronomic market and festive atmosphere even in the cold.

Local tip: Dress warmly (it's cold in February) and come hungry. Galician stew is hearty and delicious.

🍞 3. Cea Bread Festival

Where: San Cristovo de Cea, Ourense
When: Cea Bread Exaltation Festival (first Sunday of July, July 5, 2026) and Cea Bread Festival (first weekend of September, September 4-6, 2026)
What to expect: Celebration of Galicia's most famous bread (PGI). Two festivals: in July the "Exaltation" and in September the main festival. Kneading demonstrations, traditional ovens, tastings and best bread contest.

Local tip: Buy bread to take home - it keeps well and is the best gastronomic souvenir. Cea bread has Protected Geographical Indication.

🦐 4. O Grove Seafood Festival

Where: O Grove, Pontevedra
When: First half of October (October 9-18, 2026)
What to expect: 10 days of fresh seafood: barnacles, crabs, lobsters, clams... All accompanied by Albariño wine and music.

Local tip: Book in advance if you want to eat at the most famous restaurants. Or better: buy seafood at the market and have your own party.

🐙 5. Carballiño Octopus Festival

Where: O Carballiño, Ourense
When: Second Sunday of August (August 9, 2026)
What to expect: Galicia's oldest octopus festival. Thousands of kilos of octopus cooked in the streets, accompanied by wine and bread.

Local tip: Go early to avoid queues. And order the octopus "á feira", which is how it should be eaten.

🧀 6. Arzúa Cheese Festival

Where: Arzúa, A Coruña
When: First weekend of March (March 6-8, 2026)
What to expect: Celebration of Arzúa-Ulloa cheese with tastings, cheese market, contests and production demonstrations.

Local tip: Try the cheese with quince paste and a Ribeiro wine. Perfect combination.

🥧 7. Coruña Empanada Festival

Where: A Coruña
When: Mid-July (July 17-19, 2026)
What to expect: Contest for the best Galician empanada, preparation workshops, tastings and sale of empanadas of all types.

Local tip: Try the zamburiñas empanada (small scallops) - it's a unique delicacy from the Coruña coast.

🍰 8. Santiago Cake Festival

Where: Santiago de Compostela
When: July 25 (Saint James Day) and throughout the month
What to expect: Throughout July, Santiago's pastry shops compete for the best cake. On July 25 there are public tastings in Alameda Park.

Local tip: Visit several pastry shops to compare. The authentic one has almonds, eggs and sugar, nothing more.

🥓 9. Lacón con Grelos Festival

Where: Various towns in Lugo and Ourense
When: January-February (variable dates)
What to expect: The star dish of Galician winter. Gastronomic days in restaurants throughout Galicia with special lacón menus.

Local tip: It's a hearty dish, ideal for cold days. Pair it with a red wine from Ribeira Sacra.

🌶️ 10. Padrón Pepper Festival

Where: Herbón (Padrón), A Coruña
When: First weekend of August (same as Cambados)
What to expect: Celebration of the famous pepper. "Hottest peppers" contest, tastings, and of course, fried peppers for everyone.

Local tip: "Some are hot and some are not" - it's part of the fun. Try several to see your luck.

🐔 11. Vilalba Capon Fair

Where: Plaza de la Constitución, Vilalba (Lugo)
When: Around December 21 (nearest Sunday)
What to expect: Fair with almost 200 years of tradition (since 1835). Direct sale of Vilalba Capon (PGI), free tastings, awards to breeders and musical entertainment under a large tent.

Local tip: PGI capons sell out quickly - arrive early if you want to buy. Perfect for pilgrims finishing the Camino near Christmas.

For Holy Year 2026: Vilalba is on the Northern Way. In Holy Year it will be very crowded - book accommodation well in advance.

🥔 12. Coristanco Potato Festival

Where: Coristanco (Traba), A Coruña
When: Third Sunday of September (September 19-20, 2026)
What to expect: Declared Festival of Tourist Interest of Galicia. Gastronomic contests like the "Burning Potato", showcooking, parade of decorated tractors, sculptures with potatoes, popular sardine roast and live music.

Local tip: One of the first gastronomic fairs in Galicia that celebrates local produce and rural life. Try the local potatoes with grilled sardines.

Many of these festivals are free to enter, but food and drink are paid. Bring cash because some stalls don't accept cards.

Weekly Itineraries: Combine Gastronomy, Festivals and Tourism

Planning a trip to Galicia isn't just about going from festival to festival. It's about combining gastronomy with culture, tradition with tourism. Here we propose realistic one-week itineraries for different times of the year:

📅 Week 1: Late June (June 22-28, 2026)

Day 1 (Saturday): Arrival in Santiago. Welcome dinner with pulpo á feira in San Pedro neighborhood.

Day 2 (Sunday): Abastos Market in the morning. Afternoon in Alameda enjoying the first Santiago cakes of the competition.

Day 3 (Monday): Excursion to Ribadavia. Ribeiro Wine Festival (if it coincides). Visit to traditional wineries.

Day 4 (Tuesday): Route through Ribeira Sacra. Wine tastings and spectacular views of the Sil canyon.

Day 5 (Wednesday): Day in Ourense. Free thermal baths and traditional Ourense food.

Day 6 (Thursday): Return to Santiago. Special dinner with seasonal products.

Day 7 (Friday): Shopping for gastronomic souvenirs and farewell.

Star event: Start of the Santiago Cake competition in the city.

📅 Week 2: Mid-July (July 13-19, 2026)

Day 1 (Saturday): Arrival in A Coruña. Walk by the Tower of Hercules and sunset.

Day 2 (Sunday): Coruña Empanada Festival. Tasting of empanadas of all types.

Day 3 (Monday): Excursion to Betanzos. Betanceira empanada and old town.

Day 4 (Tuesday): Costa da Morte route. Fresh seafood in ports like Muxía or Camariñas.

Day 5 (Wednesday): Day in Santiago. Visit to the Cathedral without crowds (outside weekend).

Day 6 (Thursday): Excursion to Noia and Muros. Quiet beaches and fishing villages.

Day 7 (Friday): Rías Baixas Wine Festival in Cambados (if it coincides).

Perfect combination: Maritime culture + empanadas + wine.

📅 Week 3: First week of August (August 1-7, 2026)

Day 1 (Saturday): Arrival in Pontevedra. Walk through the old town and dinner at a tavern.

Day 2 (Sunday): Cambados Wine Festival (main day). Albariño, music and festive atmosphere.

Day 3 (Monday): Padrón Pepper Festival in Herbón. Fried peppers and tradition.

Day 4 (Tuesday): Beach day in Cíes Islands or on the O Grove coast.

Day 5 (Wednesday): Carballiño Octopus Festival (second Sunday, adjust dates).

Day 6 (Thursday): Albariño Route through Rías Baixas. Wineries and landscapes.

Day 7 (Friday): Day in Combarro (granaries by the sea) and farewell.

Must-see festival: The Cambados Wine Festival is the largest in Galicia.

📅 Week 4: Late August (August 22-28, 2026)

Day 1 (Saturday): Arrival in Vigo. Stone Market and ascent to Castro.

Day 2 (Sunday): Sardine Festival in some port of Rías Baixas.

Day 3 (Monday): Excursion to Baiona. Monterreal Castle and seafood.

Day 4 (Tuesday): Cruise through Vigo Bay. Cíes Islands and panoramic views.

Day 5 (Wednesday): Day in Tui. Border with Portugal and fortified cathedral.

Day 6 (Thursday): Route through Miño Valley. Quiet villages and wineries.

Day 7 (Friday): Wine shopping and farewell with seafood dinner.

Advantage: August is the month with the most festivals, but also with the most tourists.

📅 Week 5: Mid-September (September 14-20, 2026)

Day 1 (Saturday): Arrival in Ourense. Thermal baths at night under the stars.

Day 2 (Sunday): Cea Bread Festival. Freshly baked bread and demonstrations.

Day 3 (Monday): Route through Ribeira Sacra. Early harvest and autumn landscapes.

Day 4 (Tuesday): Harvest Festival in some winery in Valdeorras or Ribeira Sacra.

Day 5 (Wednesday): Excursion to Coristanco for the Potato Festival (September 19-20). Tractor parade, gastronomic contests and popular sardine roast.

Day 6 (Thursday): Return to Santiago in the afternoon. Farewell dinner.

Day 7 (Friday): Last shopping (Cea bread, wine, cheese, Coristanco potatoes) and return.

Ideal season: Fewer tourists, good weather, harvest and potato festival declared of Tourist Interest.

📅 Week 6: First week of October (October 4-10, 2026)

Day 1 (Saturday): Arrival in Pontevedra. Autumn atmosphere and gentle rains.

Day 2 (Sunday): Start of O Grove Seafood Festival. Barnacles, crabs, lobsters.

Day 3 (Monday): Full day in O Grove. Fresh seafood and coastal walks.

Day 4 (Tuesday): Excursion to Cambados (without festival, quiet). Wineries and manor houses.

Day 5 (Wednesday): Route through Arousa. Arousa Island and mussels.

Day 6 (Thursday): Day in Santiago. Museums and culture without crowds.

Day 7 (Friday): Return with canned seafood shopping and farewell. If you stay longer, in November come the Magosto festivals with roasted chestnuts and new wine.

Specialty: The best seafood of the year at more reasonable prices, and the prelude to autumn chestnut festivals.

📅 Week 7: Christmas in Galicia (December 19-25, 2026)

Day 1 (Saturday): Arrival in Santiago. Christmas market and city lights.

Day 2 (Sunday): Vilalba Capon Fair (around December 21). Purchase of PGI capon, tastings and festive Christmas atmosphere.

Day 3 (Monday): Route through Lugo. Roman wall and traditional Lugo food.

Day 4 (Tuesday): Excursion to Ribadeo. Cathedrals Beach and winter seafood.

Day 5 (Wednesday): Day in Ourense. Christmas thermal baths under the stars.

Day 6 (Thursday): Return to Santiago. Christmas Eve dinner at a traditional restaurant.

Day 7 (Friday): Christmas in Santiago. Midnight Mass (night of 24th) and Christmas lunch.

Specialty: Authentic Galician Christmas with PGI capon, winter seafood and unique traditions. Perfect for pilgrims finishing the Camino near Christmas.

These itineraries are flexible. Check the exact dates of the festivals each year and adjust according to your interests. Galicia in summer-autumn is a continuous festival, and in winter it offers a unique Christmas.

Tips to Enjoy the Festivals Like a Local

Since I was a child I've been touring gastronomic festivals throughout Galicia. While over time some festivals have disappeared and others have become crowded, these are my tips based on recent years. Some are from personal experience and others from listening to customers at the restaurant.

  1. Arrive early: The best products run out quickly, especially at popular festivals.
  2. Talk to people: Local producers love to explain their work. You'll learn a lot.
  3. Try new things: Never tried barnacles or lamprey? This is your chance.
  4. Respect traditions: Each festival has its rituals. Observe and follow the locals.
  5. Drink in moderation: Wine flows freely, but remember you need to enjoy the food.
  6. Bring cash: Many small stalls don't accept cards. Bring small bills to make change easier.
  7. Come hungry but without rushing: Enjoy every bite, every conversation, every moment.

More than Gastronomy: Complete Festivals

It's important to understand that these festivals in Galicia are not simply gastronomic events. They are full-fledged festivals where the gastronomic component is the central axis, but around it revolve concerts, orchestras, traditional dances, parades, craft markets, children's activities, and a festive atmosphere that lasts from morning until dawn.

When you go to the Cambados Wine Festival, you don't just go to taste Albariño. You go to enjoy outdoor concerts, see decorated stalls, fireworks over the estuary, and the joyful bustle of thousands of people celebrating together. The same happens at the Lalín Stew Festival, where the cold of February is fought with music, dance and the warmth of the people.

⚠️ A Living and Open Land

Galicia is not a land to get bored and stay at home or shopping in shopping centers in big cities. Galicia is a living and open land with infinity of places, traditions and festivals that will fascinate you.

Almost every Galician town has a holiday dedicated to the food product that best represents that area. It would be impossible to mention them all: from the Androlla Festival in Verín to the Botillo Festival in O Barco, passing through the Trout Festival in the rivers of Lugo, the Honey Festival in the Ourense mountains, or the Cherry Festival on the banks of the Miño.
