The Perfect Burger: Balancing Taste and Health

Published January 16, 2026 | A guide to guilt-free enjoyment

This isn't a defense of the burger. It's about reclaiming something deeper: the right to enjoy food without sacrificing your health. For years, the burger has been the scapegoat for all nutritional evils, when in reality the problem was never the burger itself, but how we've made it.

Since we opened Señarís 25 years ago, we've seen the conversation about food swing to extremes: either it's "fast food" or it's a "superfood." We believe in the middle ground—in balance. And a burger, when made consciously, perfectly represents that balance.

This article isn't just about burgers. It's about making conscious choices with every bite, about reclaiming control over what you eat, and above all, about enjoying without fear.

The Myth of "Forbidden Food"

We've created a food culture based on guilt. The burger became the symbol of everything you "shouldn't eat" if you want to stay healthy. But what if I told you that a well-made burger can be part of a balanced diet?

The problem isn't the meat, the bread, or the cheese itself. The problem is the quality of those ingredients and the amounts we consume. At Señarís, we've spent 25 years proving that you can serve a delicious burger without sacrificing the principles of conscious eating.

The Three Pillars of the Conscious Burger

1. The Meat: 100% Galician Beef from Nearby

Not all meat is the same. We work with 100% beef from calves raised in Galicia, from Carlos's Butcher Shop, located in the Plaza de Abastos. This proximity guarantees us the freshest meat, with no need for freezing or long transport.

Why isn't it meat with the Ternera Gallega designation of origin? The answer is honest: costs would rise by about 50%, and our prices are very tight so that everyone can enjoy a good burger. But that doesn't mean we skimp on quality:

  • Daily fresh meat: From the butcher shop to the counter, no middlemen.
  • Known provenance: We know where each piece comes from, something that's impossible with large distributors.
  • Real nutrition: Real meat, without additives or fillers. A 100% beef burger that not only fills you up but nourishes you.

As we say in our article about the Abastos Market, betting on local product is keeping our neighborhood's economic circle alive. Every burger we serve is a vote for that philosophy.

2. The Bread: Honesty over Marketing

Let's be clear: our bread is not artisan sourdough. It's just regular bread bought in bulk, like you'd find at any neighborhood bakery. Why? Because prices are tight and, frankly, because we believe the magic is in the meat, not the bread.

Our philosophy about bread:

  • Functionality over pretense: The bread is the vehicle, not the destination. It should be fresh, spongy, and hold the burger without falling apart.
  • Real prices: A "gourmet" bread would raise the final price, and our commitment is to keep prices accessible.
  • The main flavor is the meat: We don't want bread with too much character stealing the spotlight from the star of the show.

It's the same honesty we apply in our decision not to do delivery: we prefer to be transparent about who we are, rather than selling a false image.

3. The Sides: Conscious Choices

A burger doesn't stand alone, but the extras should add, not take away. Our approach is practical and respectful:

  • Well-made fries: Fried in high-oleic sunflower oil, changed frequently. Crispy on the outside, tender on the inside.
  • Salad as counterpoint: Fresh lettuce, tomato, onion... they're not decoration. They're freshness that balances the richness of the meat.
  • Sauces: the customer's decision: Here we're especially careful. We never put sauce directly on the burgers. We believe it's a step the customer should choose. Sauces can be an incentive, but they can also hide the true flavor of the meat. Drowning quality meat in sauce is, in our opinion, a shame. That's why we serve them separately, so everyone can decide.

The Technique: Cooking with Awareness (and Reality)

How you cook the meat makes a huge difference. We follow basic principles, but we're also honest about the realities of day-to-day work:

The 70/30 Rule

70% of the burger should be lean meat, 30% can be quality fat. This proportion guarantees juiciness without excess saturated fat. Fat is flavor, but it should be just the right amount.

Griddle Cooking: The Art of Balance

We cook on a medium-high griddle, flipping the burger just once. The goal is to sear the meat on the outside while keeping it juicy inside. Do we always get it perfect? The truth is, we don't. We're attending at the bar, sometimes there are several burgers at once, and we can't control timing with millimeter precision. But we try to keep the meat tender without letting it dry out completely on the grill. It's a constant balance between service speed and respect for the product.

Resting: When Time Allows

In theory, a burger should rest 2-3 minutes after cooking. In practice, when there's a line at the bar and people are in a hurry, sometimes that rest gets shortened. We try, but we're realistic: hospitality is a dance between the ideal and the possible.

Burgers for Everyone: The Chicken Option

We understand that not everyone wants beef all the time. That's why we offer an alternative that maintains the same spirit of quality:

Galician Chicken Burger

Free-range Galician chicken breast, also from Carlos Butcher Shop in the Plaza de Abastos. Marinated with herbs and cooked on the griddle. Lighter, equally tasty when the raw material is excellent and local.

We don't offer vegan or vegetarian options because our specialty is meat, and we prefer to do what we know best, and do it well. We don't want to offer ultra-processed substitutes that don't fit our philosophy of real, honest food.

Context Matters: Burger as Experience, Not Fuel

The perfect burger isn't just what's between the buns. It's the moment, the place, the company. At Señarís we believe in:

  • Eating seated, with time: Not standing, not rushing. Digestion begins in the mind.
  • Sharing the table: A burger tastes better when shared with good company.
  • Savoring, not gulping: Take your time. Savor each bite.

Conclusion: Your Burger, Your Decision

In the end, the perfect burger is the one you decide to build. It doesn't have to be a calorie bomb, or a "sinful" dish. It can be a balanced, nutritious, and delicious meal.

Remember: The next time you think about a burger, don't think "fast food." Think quality ingredients, conscious technique, and guilt-free enjoyment.

At Señarís we've spent 25 years proving it's possible—not because we're enlightened, but because we believe eating well isn't a luxury, it's a right. And a well-made burger is part of that right.

Your health and your palate don't have to be at war. Find the balance.

With the flavors of Galicia and respect for your well-being,
Susana and Antonio, from the griddle of Hamburguesería Señarís.
