Before you take your very first step on the Camino de Santiago, there are two documents you must understand: the Pilgrim Credential and the Compostela. This is not boring red tape. Get it wrong and you can walk hundreds of kilometres, arrive at the Cathedral and walk away with nothing — and there is no way to fix it afterwards.

Let's go step by step, from start to finish, so you have everything crystal clear long before you see the Cathedral towers.

📇 1. What is the Pilgrim Credential?

It is the official, personal and non-transferable document, known everywhere as the Camino passport. It has three main functions:

  • It identifies you as a pilgrim and gives you access to public and private albergues on the Jacobean network.
  • It is the only valid way to prove you have actually walked the route, through the stamps you collect each day.
  • It is MANDATORY and IRREPLACEABLE: without a properly stamped credential, it is impossible to get the Compostela. If you arrive in Santiago without one, or with it empty, no matter how far you have walked, there is no way to get the certificate afterwards.

⚠️ No credential = No Compostela

There are no exceptions, no workarounds, and nothing can be done "afterwards". If you arrive in Santiago without a credential or without the necessary stamps, you will not receive the Compostela under any circumstances.

✅ Where & when to get one

Ideally get it before you start, or directly at your starting point. Here are your options — the second one is the easiest if you are coming from abroad:

Option Where Price
🗺️ In your country Confraternities of St James, Camino associations, dioceses or Cathedral-authorised offices Free or €1-2 donation
🚶 At your starting point Authorised public and private albergues, parishes, churches, tourist offices and town halls at any official starting point (Sarria, Tui, Porto, León, Irún, Ferrol...) Free or €1-2 donation
📱 Digital version Credencial Digital Peregrino app (since 2025). QR stamps, legally identical to the paper version Free

⚠️ Watch out for fakes

Only credentials issued by the Cathedral or its authorised bodies are valid. Those sold in souvenir shops without official authorisation are useless.

📌 2. Usage rules: stamps, distance & validity

For your credential to be accepted at the Pilgrim Office, you must follow these very clear rules:

📏 Official minimum distance

🚶 On foot or horseback

100 km continuous on a recognised route, always finishing in Santiago.

🚲 By bicycle

200 km. Bicycles with electric assistance do NOT qualify for the Compostela.

If you start outside Spain: at least 70 km of those 100 must be within Spanish territory.
🆕 Rule change since 2025: the qualifying kilometres do NOT have to be the last 100 km immediately before Santiago — any official stretch counts. The only fixed requirement is that your final stage must end at the Cathedral.

📅 Stamp requirements

In ALL kilometres that count towards the Compostela: minimum 2 stamps per day, one at the start of your stage and one at the end, each with a clear date and place name. If they are blurred, undated or illegible, they may not be valid.

Where to stamp: albergues, churches, bars, restaurants, town halls, tourist offices… practically every place along the Camino has an official stamp.

If you walk more than 100 km, outside the qualifying distance 1 stamp per day is enough, but two never hurts.

⏸️ ⏯️ Can I pause the Camino and resume later? ✅ YES

This is one of the most common questions and one that almost no guide explains properly. According to Santiago Cathedral regulations:

✅ YES, YOU CAN STOP WHENEVER YOU LIKE AND COME BACK LATER

Resuming exactly in the same village, junction or albergue where you left off. You don't need to tell anyone or ask permission.

📋 Exact rules to keep it fully valid:

  • ⏹️ When you stop: On your final walking day, get two dated stamps at the place where you finish: one when you arrive and one before you leave, to record the exact spot and stop date.
  • ⏳ Time limit: Officially the credential has no written expiry date, but the rule the Pilgrim Office always applies and the official recommendation is to resume and complete it within 1 CALENDAR YEAR from the day you interrupted it. Beyond that, while it theoretically remains valid, they may ask for additional explanations; that's why everyone sticks to the 12-month limit.
  • ⏯️ When you return: On the first day back, the FIRST STAMP MUST BE IN THE EXACT SAME PLACE where you left off, with the new date. This proves you haven't skipped any kilometres.
  • 📄 Same document: You must use THE SAME CREDENTIAL throughout. If you lose it along the way, previous stamps are considered lost and you have to start over.
  • 🧭 Order: Stages must always follow chronological and geographical order, without jumping from one route to another or going backwards.

🧩 Practical example

You start in Sarria, walk 4 days to Arzúa, and have to go home for work or health reasons. You keep the credential safe. 8 months later you go back to Arzúa, stamp there first, walk the remaining 39 km to Santiago and meet all requirements → you get the Compostela with no problem at all.

💡 Real fact: many people walk the entire 800 km French Way over 3 or 4 different summers, stopping and returning each year, and it is completely accepted and common.

🧾 3. What is the Compostela?

Officially it is the certificate issued by the Cathedral Chapter, written in Latin, which certifies that you have completed the pilgrimage fulfilling all the rules. There are two types, both completely free:

Type Reason Features
✅ Compostela Religious, spiritual or inner search Historic document in Latin. The best known.
📄 Distance Certificate Tourist, sport or cultural Same official validity, without the Latin religious formula.

I've got a few of these in a drawer at home — minus one. Let me be straight with you: this piece of paper won't get you a job, it doesn't entitle you to any lifelong pension, it won't change your life overnight or make you a better person than anyone else.

What it does do is hold onto memory. Years go by, you open that drawer one random day, you see them there, and suddenly everything floods back. I myself walked into a lost village church one afternoon to get a stamp and met the love of my life. Two months later we set off on the Camino together… and she ran off with a tall blond German, nearly two metres tall. Never saw her again. As we say here in Galicia: what the Camino gives, the Camino takes away 😂.

I also remember that Korean pilgrim, barely a word in common between us, with whom I walked two whole days in relentless rain, cheering each other on almost without speaking. The cold, the burning sun, the laughter in the albergue, the foot pain that only more walking seemed to cure…

That's what really matters. The Compostela is just the paper, but it's also the key that unlocks memories you yourself had already forgotten, and the moment you touch it they come flooding back, just like that, as if it were yesterday. That's the part they never print in the official handbooks.

⚙️ 4. How to collect it in Santiago: 2026 process — NO MORE QUEUES!

📍 Current address

Oficina de Acogida al Peregrino (Pilgrim Office)
Rúa Carretas, 33
(5 minutes' walk from Obradoiro Square; it is NO LONGER at Rúa do Vilar)

🕒 Opening hours

  • April – October: 08:00 – 21:00
  • November – March: 09:00 – 20:00
  • Sundays & public holidays: 10:00 – 20:00
  • Closed only: 25 December & 1 January

✅ Mobile phone queue system, since 2024

No more standing in line for 2 hours:

  1. On arrival, take a ticket with a QR code from the machines at the entrance.
  2. Scan it with your phone and see your position in real time.
  3. You receive an SMS or push notification when it's your turn. Meanwhile you can rest, grab a coffee, or wander around.
  4. When you go in: just present your credential, state your reason and in 5-10 minutes you have it in your hand. They almost never ask for ID or passport, only if there is some exceptional doubt.

⚠️ 5. Mistakes that leave you WITHOUT the Compostela, no remedy

I see these all the time and they are completely avoidable:

  • Arriving without a credential or with it empty / barely any stamps → NO SOLUTION.
  • Stamps without date, without place name, or illegible.
  • Fewer than 2 stamps per day in the qualifying kilometres.
  • Stopping and restarting at a different point, without stamping in the same place.
  • Using an electric bicycle.
  • Getting all or almost all stamps on the last day.

📌 6. Extra certificates

If you continue to the coast after Santiago:

⛵ Finisterre → Finisterrana

89 km from Santiago. Issued in Finisterre, not at the Santiago Pilgrim Office.

⛵ Muxía → Muxiana

Issued in Muxía, not in Santiago.

✨ Tips Only for English-speaking Pilgrims

After 25 years, I know exactly where you trip up:

  1. 🇪🇸 Siesta is REAL: 13:00–17:00 most small shops, banks, chemists and supermarkets SHUT. English guides almost never say this clearly. Plan water and food before 13:00.
  2. Old office address: every YouTube video and blog from before 2024 says Rúa do Vilar. It is wrong now.
  3. Emergency 112: operators speak excellent English 24/7, free call.
  4. Stamping in bars is fine: you do not have to buy anything, though a coffee or water is polite and costs €1-2.
  5. "Pilgrim Menu": €10-14, 3 courses + wine + bread. Heavy as lead. Do not eat one at 14:00 in 35°C heat or you will not move for 4 hours.
  6. Albergues: municipal ones = NO booking, turn up by 13:00. Private = book 2-3 months ahead in July/August.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

How much does the Credential cost?

The official credential is free at most authorised points. Some associations or albergues request a €1-2 donation to cover printing costs.

How much does the Compostela cost?

It is completely free. The Pilgrim Office charges nothing for issuing the Compostela or Distance Certificate. If someone offers to "arrange" it for money, it's a scam.

What if I lose my Credential halfway?

Previous stamps are considered lost. You'll need a new credential and must start recording stamps again from that point. This may mean you don't meet the minimum distance requirement.

Can I get the Compostela if I do the Camino on an e-bike?

No. Electric-assist bicycles (pedelec, e-bike) do not qualify for the Compostela. Only conventional bicycles are accepted.

Do I need to bring my passport or ID to collect the Compostela?

Normally they don't ask for it. It's only needed in exceptional cases if there is any doubt about the pilgrim's identity or the validity of the credential.

💡 A final thought

The Camino is above all about the experience, the kilometres walked and everything you live and feel along the way. The Compostela is just the paper that certifies it. But following these few clear rules is all you need so that, many years from now, when you open that drawer, you have your key to live it all over again.
