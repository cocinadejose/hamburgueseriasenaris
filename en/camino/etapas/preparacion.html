Stage 0: The Final Preparation in Santiago or Sarria

The day that defines your Camino. By the team at Señarís Burger Bar.

Why Start in Sarria? The 100km Secret

First things first, let's solve the big question: why does almost everyone start in Sarria? The answer is simple and strategic. Sarria is located about **112 kilometers from Santiago**. To obtain the "Compostela," the official certificate that proves your pilgrimage, you need to have walked at least the last 100 kilometers. Sarria is, therefore, the perfect and most popular starting point to meet this requirement.

This also means that from here on, the route becomes busier and the pilgrim atmosphere intensifies. Get ready to meet people from all over the world!

Pilgrim Logistics: Santiago or Sarria First?

This is the most important logistical decision you will make. Your arrival point (Santiago airport, Sarria train station...) will define your "Stage 0." Let's analyze the two main scenarios.

Plan A: You Arrive at Santiago Airport (SCQ)

If your plane lands in Santiago, your strategy will depend on your arrival time and your level of fatigue.

Option 1: You arrive in the morning or at noon (The ideal one!)

You have plenty of time. Our golden recommendation is: **go directly to Sarria on the same day**. Take a bus from the airport to the Santiago bus station, and from there, another one to Sarria. You will arrive in the afternoon, with time to settle in, have dinner, and rest.

  • Why not stay in Santiago? Because logistically, it's a mess. You would have to pay for a hotel or albergue for just one night, carry your backpack around the city, and get up early the next day anyway to go to Sarria. It's better to sleep at the starting point.

Option 2: You arrive late or are very tired

If your flight arrives at night and there are no more buses, or if the trip has left you exhausted, don't complicate things. **Sleep in Santiago and travel to Sarria the next morning.** There are very early buses, precisely because of the large influx of pilgrims. But beware, this has an important consequence:

Beware of the heat and overcrowding! If you travel to Sarria in the morning, you will start walking later. In summer, this means doing it under a scorching sun. In addition, in recent years the Camino has become very crowded. Many albergues do not accept reservations and assign places on a first-come, first-served basis. Starting late can mean not finding a place. This is no joke!

Plan B: You Arrive Directly in Sarria (by Train or Bus)

Sarria, despite not having an airport, is very well connected precisely because it is the main starting point of the Camino. There are **direct buses from Madrid and other major cities**, as well as a busy train line.

If this is your case, the advice is the same: **do not start walking on the same day of your arrival**. Use that afternoon to acclimatize.

  • Get your Credential: Your number one priority. You will easily find it at the Church of Santa Mariña, the Church of El Salvador, or in the main albergues.
  • Prepare supplies: In Sarria, there are many supermarkets and local shops where you can buy water, fruit, or nuts for the first stage.
  • Dine and rest: Start the Camino the next day with renewed energy, not like a zombie. You will enjoy it twice as much!

Two Golden Rules and an Ace up the Sleeve

1. Don't Forget the Stamps ("Cuños")!

For your Compostela to be valid, you must stamp your Credential **a minimum of twice a day** throughout the route from Sarria. You will find stamps everywhere: albergues, churches, bars, town halls... Turn it into a fun little ritual!

2. The Problem of Full Albergues

Overcrowding is real. If you are worried about not finding a place, the only solution is to get up early and be one of the first to arrive at your destination. Or, if you prefer peace and quiet, consider booking private albergues or pensions that do allow it, even if it is a little more expensive.

3. Our Ace up the Sleeve

And if after all this mess of schedules and albergues, what you need is a quick solution to get to Sarria on time... well, my husband will always be there with his taxi and a smile waiting for you. Hahaha!
